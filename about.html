Our story

Built by a small clinic. Grown by the people who stayed.

VitalCare opened in 1987 as a 40-bed community clinic on Ridgeway Avenue. Almost four decades later, the address hasn't changed — but we now treat over 90,000 patients a year across 36 specialties. What hasn't changed either: the belief that good care is unhurried care.

Our mission

To deliver medicine that is rigorous in its science and generous in its attention — treating every person who walks through our doors as someone with a life to get back to, not a case number.

Our approach

Coordinated care teams instead of siloed departments. A physician who sees your ER visit knows what your cardiologist knows. It's a small structural choice that changes almost everything about the experience.

Four decades, in brief

A short history of how a 40-bed clinic became a regional hospital.

1987

VitalCare opens on Ridgeway Avenue

A 40-bed community clinic founded by Dr. Eleanor Marsh, focused on family medicine and maternity care.

1996

First cardiology and neurology wings added

Growing demand from the surrounding county prompts the hospital's first major expansion.

2005

Level II trauma center designation

VitalCare becomes the county's primary trauma receiving facility, open 24 hours a day.

2014

Level III NICU and maternity tower completed

A dedicated seven-floor tower brings maternity, NICU and pediatrics under one roof.

2023

412 beds, 36 specialties

VitalCare reaches its current scale while keeping the same care-team model it started with.
